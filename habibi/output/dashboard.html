Lesson of the week: Stops are insurance, not predictions
The emergency stop sits about 3 normal daily swings below the entry. It is there for disasters, so it should rarely fire.
The rules, in plain English
  1. Two parts. 30% in VOO (the S&P 500), held long term and topped up toward its target from cash at weekly reviews. The other 70% is the active portfolio, reviewed once a week.
  2. Weekly review (Monday). Every stock and sector ETF on the list is ranked by risk-adjusted momentum: its 6-, 3- and 1-month gains divided by how much it normally swings. Every stock must also pass a business-quality check (growing revenue, expected profits, sane valuation). We hold the top 5, at most 2 per sector and at most 3 across tech + chips combined, and keep a holding while it stays in the top 8. Decisions use Friday's close; you place orders at Monday's open.
  3. Size by swings. Jumpier names get fewer dollars, so each holding can hurt about the same. No holding is more than 30% of the active part.
  4. Exits. Sell at the weekly review if it falls out of the top 8, or closes below its 50-day average two weeks in a row. Sell half at +25% and trail the rest. A rank exit needs 1 weekly review(s) in a row outside the top 8. Emergency stop 3 normal daily swings below entry (at most 30%). Between reviews, a market-wide drop never triggers a sale (only the emergency stop can). At the weekly review a holding outside the top 8 is sold even after a market-wide drop: rank is relative, so it means other names are holding up better. Waiting an extra week tested worse.
  5. Quality dips (20% of the account). Great businesses on sale: business-quality score 80+, at least 20% below the 1-year high, and cheap for their growth (forward P/E divided by revenue growth under 1.0). Up to 3 names (at most 2 per sector; obvious cyclical peaks with a forward P/E under 6 or revenue growth over 150% are skipped), at most 7% of the account each, bought in two halves (the second once it closes back above its 50-day average or after 20 trading days). Held 6-12 months; sold only if the business story breaks (quality below 60 or shrinking revenue), at the -35% disaster stop, or half at +40%. Not bought while the market light is RED or the account loss limit is hit. Log these fills with the note "value". This part is not backtested: free data has no history of fundamentals, so it has its own scoreboard line.
  6. Market light. RED (S&P 500 below its 200-day average or fear spiking) = no new buys. Holdings follow their own rules.
  7. Safety limits. Down 12% since the new plan began = no new buys. Down 18% = full review together. Nothing is ever sold automatically just because the account is down.
  8. New cash goes in over 3 weekly batches. No buys within 3 trading days of an earnings report.
Glossary
MomentumHow much a price has risen over recent months. Winners tend to keep winning for a while.
ATRAverage True Range: the size of a normal day's move, in dollars. Used to set stops and sizes.
StopA pre-set price where we sell to cap the damage if something goes badly wrong.
50-day / 200-day averageThe average price over the last 50 / 200 trading days. Above = uptrend.
BetaHow much a stock usually moves when the S&P 500 moves 1%. Beta 1.5 = moves about 1.5%.
RebalanceBringing each part of the account back to its target share.
Sector ETFOne fund that holds a whole sector (e.g. XLE = US energy companies).
CDRCanadian Depositary Receipt: a CAD-priced, currency-hedged version of a US stock on the Canadian exchange.
DrawdownHow far the account is below its highest value.
VIXThe market's 'fear gauge'. Below 20 is calm; above 28 is stressed.
