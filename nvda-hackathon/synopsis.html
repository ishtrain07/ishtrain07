Mentioned (working name)

Customers now ask AI who to hire. Mentioned tells a small business whether AI recommends it, and how to fix it if not.

The problem

People increasingly ask an AI assistant instead of scrolling Google: "best bakery near me", "plumber open now in Brampton". The AI gives one answer naming 3-5 businesses. If a business isn't named, it never gets the call. Big brands pay for tools that track this. A café, plumber or dental clinic has no idea where it stands, or why.

How it helps an SME (concrete example)

Maria's Bakery, Toronto. She types in her name, city and "bakery". Two minutes later:
  • Score: AI mentions her in 3 of 25 customer questions. A competitor appears in 19.
  • Why: the competitor lists prices and hours, has an FAQ answering "gluten-free?", and is on 4 local directories Maria isn't.
  • Fixes, ready to paste: FAQ text for her site, a better Google Business description, structured data her web person can drop in, a review-request text for her customers, and the 4 directories to claim.
  • Weekly re-check: "You now appear in 9 of 25 answers."
She gets something to do in an afternoon, not a report she can't act on.

How it works

  1. Ask: NVIDIA Nemotron Nano writes 25 realistic customer questions.
  2. Search: Tavily searches the live web for each one, the way AI assistants do.
  3. Answer: Nemotron Super answers each question from those sources and names businesses.
  4. Diagnose: Nemotron Ultra compares the business with the winners and explains the gap.
  5. Fix: it generates paste-ready fixes. A weekly re-scan runs on Nebius Serverless Jobs.

Runs on Nebius Token Factory + Serverless Endpoints. It is a transparent, repeatable panel that shows the sources behind every answer. We do not claim to show ChatGPT's exact answer.

Scope & potential

  • Large market: almost every local business depends on being found, and AI answers are becoming a front door.
  • Recurring value: AI answers change, so a monthly monitoring subscription is natural, like SEO tools today.
  • Channel: marketing agencies and web designers who serve hundreds of SMEs can resell it.
  • Expands to: multi-location chains, e-commerce products ("best running shoes under $100"), and tracking which fixes actually moved the score.

Honest risks

  • Hard to prove it brings revenue. V1 shows visibility, not sales.
  • SEO incumbents will add "AI visibility" features. Our edge is SME-simple and fix-first.
  • A simulated answer can differ from a real assistant's, so we show sources and confidence.

Backup ideas (if we don't go with Mentioned)

Curb Cut: website accessibility fixer48 / 60

Point it at a small business website. It finds accessibility failures, fixes the actual code (alt text, labels, contrast), and proves each fix by re-running the audit.

SME angle: small businesses get accessibility legal demand letters and lock out disabled customers. Strength: a measurable before/after score, and it matches the Coding track brief exactly. Weakness: most crowded track; less visual.

Shop Brain: owner's know-how → staff training app46 / 60

The owner talks through "how we open the shop" for 5 minutes. It becomes step-by-step checklists, a quiz for new hires, and answers in the staff member's own language.

SME angle: high staff turnover, and training lives in the owner's head. Strength: very visual and human. Weakness: needs speech-to-text and more UI; parts feel like a chatbot.

Invoice Watch: catches supplier overcharges45 / 60

Snap supplier invoices. It catches price creep, short deliveries and new fees, then drafts the credit request.

SME angle: thin restaurant margins, and nobody checks invoices line by line. Strength: clear dollar figure recovered. Weakness: reading photos is error-prone; needs real invoices.
